Core setup

0/4 All set

Your foundation: bank account, Roth IRA, budget app, and a W-4 you actually understand.

  • Bank account: Get a checking account you control (no surprise fees). Add a savings bucket for emergencies, even if you start small.
  • Roth IRA: If you have earned income, learn how a Roth works (money grows tax-free for retirement). Even small, regular deposits add up.
  • Budget app: Pick one method you will actually open (app or spreadsheet). Track what comes in and what must go out before fun spending.
  • W-4 checklist: When you start a job, your W-4 tells the employer how much tax to withhold. Getting it wrong means a big refund or a surprise bill. Use the IRS estimator or your campus career or VITA resource when you are unsure.

Building credit from zero

0/4 All set

No history yet? No drama. Here is how to start strong without falling for myths.

  • Secured card: A common first step is a secured credit card with a small limit you back with a deposit. Use it lightly and pay the full statement balance on time.
  • Authorized user: Sometimes a parent or trusted person adds you to their card. It can help your file, but their habits become yours on paper. Only do this with someone who pays on time and keeps balances low.
  • Pay on time, every time: Payment history is the biggest piece of your score. Set reminders or autopay for at least the minimum, ideally the full balance.
  • Myth busted: You do not need to carry a balance to build credit. Paying in full is fine.

Know where you are

0/3 All set

Net worth plus a simple budget split: the GPS for your money.

  • Net worth: List what you own (cash, car value, investments) minus what you owe (loans, card balances). One simple spreadsheet is enough. Update it once a month or each semester.
  • Budgeting tools: Same app or sheet as core setup works here. Split spending into fixed (rent, phone, subscriptions) and flexible (food, transit, fun).
  • Why it matters: You cannot steer if you do not know the starting point. Numbers remove guesswork when you decide what to cut or what to fund next.

Buying your first car

0/4 All set

Before you fall in love at the lot, run the real numbers and keep your power at the table.

  • Total cost: Monthly payment is not the whole story. Add insurance, gas, maintenance, registration, and parking.
  • Pre-approval: If you borrow, compare rates from a credit union or bank before you fall in love with a car at the lot.
  • Inspection and history: For used cars, a prepurchase inspection and a vehicle history report are worth the fee.
  • Negotiation: Walk in with a max price and be willing to leave. Sleep on big decisions.

Renting your first apartment

0/4 All set

Leases, surprise costs, insurance, and roommate peace treaties. Read this before you sign.

  • Read the lease: Know rent due date, late fees, who pays utilities, subletting rules, and how notice works when you move out.
  • Upfront costs: Budget for security deposit, first month, sometimes last month, and broker fees if your market uses them.
  • Renters insurance: Often cheap and sometimes required. It covers your stuff and liability if something goes wrong.
  • Roommates: Put shared rules in writing (splitting bills, guests, quiet hours) before tension shows up.
