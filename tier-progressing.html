Housing

0/5 All set

The rent-vs-buy decision and making your housing build equity.

  • Rent vs buy math: Run the numbers, not the emotions. Compare total monthly ownership cost (mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance at 1% of home value per year, HOA) against renting plus investing the difference. The NY Times rent-vs-buy calculator is a solid starting point.
  • Down payment strategy: Aim for 20% to avoid PMI (private mortgage insurance, which adds $100 to $300 a month for no equity benefit). If that takes another year of saving, it is almost always worth the wait.
  • Location as investment: Buy in an area with strong job growth, good schools, and rising demand. Your home is not just shelter; it is likely your largest single asset. Do not buy in a neighborhood you would not want to live in for 5 or more years.
  • Maintenance reserves: Budget 1% to 2% of the home's value per year for repairs. On a $350K home, that is $290 to $580 a month. Skipping this leads to deferred maintenance that crushes resale value.
  • House hacking: Consider buying a duplex or a place with a rentable basement or spare room. The rental income can cover a significant portion of your mortgage and accelerate your wealth building.

Transportation

0/5 All set

Optimize your ride for total cost of ownership, not monthly payment.

  • New vs certified pre-owned: A 1 to 2 year old certified pre-owned vehicle gives you warranty protection with 20% to 30% less depreciation. The "new car smell" costs real money.
  • Total cost thinking: Monthly payment is the wrong metric. Calculate 5-year total cost: purchase price minus resale value, plus insurance, gas, maintenance. A cheaper car with expensive repairs can cost more overall.
  • Strategic car-light living: If you work remotely or hybrid, consider whether you need a car at all. Ride-share plus occasional rental can be cheaper than ownership if you drive under 5,000 miles per year.
  • Umbrella insurance: At this net worth level, add an umbrella policy ($1M coverage is usually $150 to $300 per year). It protects you beyond your auto policy limits in a serious accident.
  • Save the car payment: When you pay off a car, keep making that payment into a "next car fund." In 3 to 4 years, you can pay cash for your next vehicle and skip interest entirely.

Lifestyle & Food

0/5 All set

Scale your life without letting lifestyle creep eat your wealth.

  • The lifestyle creep trap: Every raise feels like permission to spend more. Instead, commit to saving at least 50% of every raise before adjusting your lifestyle. Automate the savings so it never hits your checking account.
  • Food as investment: Shift from "eating out because it is easy" to "eating out because it is worth it." Cook most meals, but invest in 2 to 3 genuinely great dining experiences per month instead of 15 mediocre ones.
  • The 72-hour rule: For any non-essential purchase over $100, wait 72 hours. If you still want it and it fits your budget, buy it. Most impulse purchases fade in that window.
  • Travel planning: You can afford to travel now. Budget for it intentionally: a separate travel fund with automatic transfers. Off-peak flights, points strategies, and house-sitting can stretch travel budgets significantly.
  • Values audit: Once a quarter, review your spending against your stated values. If you say health matters but spend $0 on it and $500 on delivery food, something is misaligned.
