What Was Identified About TAP

These are the main organizational findings from the discussion, translated into clear board-level takeaways.

1. Our mission is stronger than our current wording

The underlying idea is compelling: people should be able to build wealth, protect it, and leave more behind than they started with. The phrase “leaving a net positive” gives us a simple strategic anchor.

2. Our audience needs to be tighter

Trying to serve “everyone” makes fundraising and communications less effective. The recommended center of gravity is young professionals up to roughly age 45, with students as a secondary audience.

3. We have programs, but not yet enough consolidated proof

We can point to talks, events, scholarships, mentorship, and community reach. The missing piece is a single view of participation plus outcome-based proof such as referrals made, jobs or internships secured, financial actions taken, or scholarship alumni progress.

4. We should not rush into large grants

Institutional grants come with reporting and stewardship obligations. Before scaling grant applications, TAP needs stronger budgets, consistent metrics, clearer program definitions, and enough capacity to manage awarded funds well.

Board takeaway: The issue is not whether TAP has substance. It does. The issue is packaging that substance into a clear, measurable, fundable nonprofit story.

Current Program Portfolio

Tamar’s review reinforced that these programs are the foundation of TAP’s current impact story. The next step is to define the purpose and measurable outcomes of each one.

TAP Network

Volunteer professionals connect students and young professionals with mentorship, referrals, introductions, and career guidance.

Potential metrics: introductions made, mentor matches, referrals, internship and job outcomes.

TAP Talks

Short educational intros followed by open discussion, with topics tailored to five net-worth stages.

Potential metrics: attendance, repeat attendance, post-session actions, content completion.

In-Person Events

Community-building events such as the Brooklyn kickoff and Intention Brunches focused on goals, accountability, and peer support.

Potential metrics: registrations, attendance, donor conversion, connections formed.

Legacy Scholarship

Annual scholarships for students plus a lunch that connects recipients, parents, and alumni.

Potential metrics: dollars awarded, recipients, graduation progress, internships, alumni engagement.

Future Partner Expansion

Long-term ambition to extend TAP through trusted partners in multiple U.S. cities rather than relying only on a centralized team.

Future metrics: partner cities, active volunteers, local programming, people served.

Recommended Roadmap

The immediate goal is not “apply for every grant.” It is to build the minimum operating system that lets TAP pursue funding with credibility and manage it responsibly.

1
Clarify the message Align the website, donor materials, and program descriptions around who we serve, what we do, where we operate, and the “leaving a net positive” mission.
2
Consolidate impact data Build one master tally across TAP Talks, Zeffy, email, scholarships, and the Network. Add outcome metrics, not only attendance.
3
Create simple program budgets Document current spend, realistic program costs, and 1 to 3 year projections. Be ready to explain exactly what new funding would unlock.
4
Use common grant applications as practice Draft strong reusable answers for mission, population served, geography, need, programming, impact, funding history, and use of funds.
5
Pursue right-sized funding Start with grants and partners appropriate for TAP’s current scale. Grow into larger institutional opportunities as reporting capacity and proof of impact improve.
6
Build stewardship into the operating model For every grant or larger donation accepted, define who tracks spending, who reports outcomes, and how funders will be updated.
