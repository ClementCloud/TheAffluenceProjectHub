Benefits Breakdown

Your salary is only part of the picture. Here's how to decode the rest of your compensation.

Beyond the Base Salary

When you see a job offer, the number on the page is just the starting point. Total compensation includes everything your employer puts toward you, and it can add 20–40% on top of your base pay.

  • 💰
    Base Pay Your fixed annual salary before taxes. This is what most people focus on, but it's rarely the whole story. Always ask about total comp.
  • 🎯
    Bonuses Annual, quarterly, or signing bonuses can add 5–20% of your base. Ask if bonuses are guaranteed or performance-based, and what the realistic payout history looks like.
  • 📈
    Equity & Stock Options RSUs, stock options, or profit-sharing can be worth thousands per year. At some companies, equity makes up more than half of total comp for senior roles.
  • 🤝
    Signing Bonus A one-time payment to offset what you're leaving behind. Typically $2K–$30K depending on your level. Watch for clawback clauses: many require you to stay 12–24 months or repay it.

Health Insurance Decoded

Health insurance is often your most valuable benefit. Employer contributions can be worth $7,000–$20,000 per year. But not all plans are equal.

  • 🏥
    PPO (Preferred Provider Organization) Higher premiums, but you can see any doctor without a referral. Best if you want flexibility or have existing relationships with specialists.
  • 🔒
    HMO (Health Maintenance Organization) Lower premiums, but you need a primary care doctor and referrals for specialists. Works well if you're fine staying in-network and want lower monthly costs.
  • 🛡️
    HDHP (High-Deductible Health Plan) Lowest premiums, highest deductible. You pay more out-of-pocket before insurance kicks in, but you unlock access to an HSA, one of the best tax-advantaged accounts available.
  • 📋
    Key Terms to Compare Premium = your monthly cost. Deductible = what you pay before insurance covers anything. Out-of-pocket max = the most you'll spend in a year. Copay = fixed fee per visit. Compare all four across plans.
💡
Pro Tip

If you're generally healthy and under 30, an HDHP paired with an HSA can save you thousands per year. You'll pay less in premiums, get triple tax advantages on your HSA contributions, and can invest the balance for long-term growth.

Retirement Benefits (401k & More)

Your employer's retirement plan is one of the most powerful wealth-building tools you have. The math is simple: free money + tax advantages + compound growth = serious wealth over time.

  • 🏦
    Employer Match = Free Money If your employer matches 50% of contributions up to 6% of your salary, that's a guaranteed 3% raise you're leaving on the table by not contributing. A $70K salary with a 3% match means $2,100/year free.
  • 📅
    Vesting Schedules Some employer matches vest over 3–5 years. If you leave before you're fully vested, you forfeit part of the match. Check your vesting schedule. It factors into your "stay vs. leave" decision.
  • ⚖️
    Traditional vs. Roth 401k Traditional = tax break now, pay taxes in retirement. Roth = pay taxes now, withdraw tax-free later. If you're early in your career and in a lower tax bracket, Roth often wins. If you're in a high bracket, traditional may save you more.
  • 🔑
    IRA as a Backup If your employer doesn't offer a 401k or the plan fees are high, you can open a Traditional or Roth IRA on your own. The 2026 contribution limit is $7,000 ($8,000 if you're 50+).
⚡
Non-Negotiable Rule

Always contribute at least enough to get the full employer match. Not doing so is literally declining free money. Even if you have debt, the instant 50–100% return on matched contributions almost always beats paying down low-interest loans first.

HSA: The Triple Tax Advantage

The Health Savings Account is the only account in the U.S. tax code with a triple tax benefit: tax-deductible contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free withdrawals for medical expenses.

  • 1️⃣
    Tax Benefit #1: Deductible Contributions Every dollar you contribute reduces your taxable income, just like a traditional 401k. In 2026, you can contribute up to $4,300 individually or $8,550 for a family.
  • 2️⃣
    Tax Benefit #2: Tax-Free Growth Once your balance hits a threshold (often $1,000–$2,000), you can invest it in index funds. Those investments grow completely tax-free: no capital gains taxes, ever.
  • 3️⃣
    Tax Benefit #3: Tax-Free Withdrawals Use the funds for qualified medical expenses and you pay zero tax on withdrawals. After age 65, you can withdraw for any purpose. It's just taxed as regular income (like a traditional IRA).
  • 🧠
    The Stealth Retirement Account Strategy Pay medical expenses out-of-pocket now, save your receipts, invest the HSA money, and let it grow for decades. Withdraw the reimbursement tax-free at any point in the future. There's no time limit on reimbursement.
  • ✅
    Who Qualifies You must be enrolled in a qualifying High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). You can't be covered by another non-HDHP plan, be enrolled in Medicare, or be claimed as a dependent.

Equity & Stock Options

Equity compensation can be life-changing, or confusing. Here's what the different types actually mean and how to think about them.

  • 📊
    RSUs (Restricted Stock Units) The most common type at public companies. You're given shares that vest over time (typically 4 years). When they vest, they're taxed as ordinary income at their market value. Simple: you get shares, you sell or hold.
  • 🎟️
    ISOs (Incentive Stock Options) Common at startups. You get the right to buy shares at a set "strike price." If the company grows, the gap between your strike price and market price is your profit. Tax advantage: if you hold 1+ year after exercise and 2+ years after grant, gains are taxed at long-term capital gains rates.
  • 🛒
    ESPP (Employee Stock Purchase Plan) Buy company stock at a 5–15% discount through payroll deductions. At many companies, this is essentially a guaranteed 15% return. Max contribution is typically $25,000/year in stock value.
  • ⏳
    Vesting Cliff Most equity grants have a 1-year cliff: you get nothing if you leave before 12 months. After the cliff, shares typically vest monthly or quarterly over the remaining 3 years. Factor this into any job change decision.
  • 📉
    When to Sell General rule: don't let company stock become more than 10–15% of your net worth. Diversification protects you. Selling as shares vest and reinvesting in index funds reduces concentration risk.

Other Benefits Worth Knowing

Beyond health and retirement, your benefits package likely includes perks that can save you hundreds or thousands annually, if you actually use them.

  • 🏖️
    PTO Policies Some companies have "unlimited" PTO, which often means people take less time off. Ask what the average number of days taken actually is. Accrued PTO may be paid out when you leave; unlimited PTO won't be.
  • 👶
    Parental Leave Paid parental leave varies wildly, from 0 weeks at some companies to 20+ weeks at others. This can be worth $10,000–$40,000 in income. Ask about it even if you're not planning a family soon.
  • 🎓
    Tuition Reimbursement Many employers offer $5,250/year tax-free for education expenses (the IRS limit). Some cover full graduate degrees. This benefit often goes unused, so check if yours offers it.
  • 🚌
    Commuter Benefits Pre-tax transit and parking benefits let you save on commuting costs with money taken out before taxes. The limit is about $325/month for transit and $325/month for parking.
  • 🛡️
    Life & Disability Insurance Most employers provide basic life insurance (1–2x salary) and short/long-term disability for free. Supplemental coverage is often cheaper through your employer than buying it privately.
  • 💪
    Wellness Stipends Gym memberships, mental health apps, wellness reimbursements: these are increasingly common. Even $50–$100/month adds up. Check your benefits portal; many people don't realize they have these.
