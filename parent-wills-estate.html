Wills & Estate Basics

Nobody wants to think about this. But the people you love will thank you for planning ahead.

Why You Need a Will (Even if You're Young)

If you have children, you need a will. Period. It's not about how much money you have. It's about who makes decisions when you can't.

  • ⚖️
    Without a will, the state decidesIntestacy laws vary, but the result is the same: a judge, not you, decides who gets your assets and who raises your kids. The process is slow, expensive, and public.
  • 👶
    Guardianship is the real reasonFor parents, the most important thing a will does is name who raises your children. Without one, a court picks, and family members may fight over it.
  • 🎯
    It's about control and clarityA will isn't morbid. It's a gift to the people you leave behind. It removes confusion, prevents conflict, and ensures your wishes are honored, not guessed at.
  • ⏰
    Young doesn't mean invincibleAccidents and illness don't wait for retirement age. If you own anything or love anyone, a will protects them right now, not someday.

The Essential Documents

A will is the starting point, but a complete estate plan has four pieces. Each one handles a different scenario.

  • 📜
    Last Will and TestamentSays who gets your stuff and who raises your kids. It only takes effect after death, and it goes through probate (court review).
  • 🏥
    Living Will (Healthcare Directive)Spells out your medical wishes if you can't speak for yourself: life support, resuscitation, pain management. This isn't about death, it's about dignity.
  • 🔑
    Durable Power of AttorneyNames someone to handle your finances if you're incapacitated: paying bills, managing accounts, filing taxes. Without it, your family needs a court order.
  • 🩺
    Healthcare ProxyNames someone to make medical decisions on your behalf. Different from a living will; the proxy fills in the gaps for situations you didn't anticipate.
💡
Real talk on cost: A basic will costs $200–$500 through an attorney or as little as $50 online through services like Trust & Will or FreeWill. Not having one costs your family thousands in stress, legal fees, and court time. This is one of the cheapest forms of protection you can buy.

Naming a Guardian

This is the conversation most parents avoid, and the one that matters most. Don't assume family members already know your wishes.

  • 💜
    Choose by values, not obligationThe best guardian shares your parenting values, has emotional stability, and actually wants the responsibility. Don't default to the oldest sibling because it feels expected.
  • 💰
    Consider financial readinessA guardian doesn't need to be rich, but they should be stable. Life insurance proceeds and your estate can help fund the care, so put that in writing too.
  • 🔄
    Name alternatesYour first choice might not be able or willing when the time comes. Name a backup, and ideally a third option. Update every few years or after major life changes.
  • 🗣️
    Have the actual conversationDon't surprise someone with guardianship in a document they've never seen. Ask them directly, explain your reasoning, and confirm they're willing.

Beneficiary Designations

Here's a fact most people don't know: beneficiary designations override your will. If your will says one thing and your 401(k) beneficiary form says another, the form wins.

  • 🏦
    Check every accountBank accounts, 401(k), IRA, life insurance policies, brokerage accounts, HSA. Each one has its own beneficiary form. Review them all.
  • 📅
    Review yearlyMarriage, divorce, new children, death of a beneficiary. Any life change means your forms may be outdated. Put a yearly review on your calendar.
  • 📋
    POD and TOD accountsPayable-on-Death (bank) and Transfer-on-Death (brokerage) designations let assets pass directly to a named person without probate. Simple, free, and hugely effective.
  • ⚠️
    Don't name minors directlyIf you list a child as beneficiary, a court will appoint a guardian to manage the money until they're 18, and then they get it all at once. Use a trust or custodial account instead.

Life Insurance Basics

If anyone depends on your income, you need life insurance. It's not complicated once you know the basics.

  • 📊
    Term vs. whole lifeTerm insurance covers you for a set period (20 or 30 years) at a low cost. Whole life is permanent but costs 5–15x more. For most families, term is the clear winner; it covers the years your dependents need you most.
  • 🧮
    How much do you need?A common rule: 10–12x your annual income. A family earning $80K should have $800K–$960K in coverage. Factor in mortgage payoff, childcare costs, and college funding.
  • ⏳
    Buy it youngA healthy 30-year-old can get a $500K 20-year term policy for $20–$30/month. Wait until 45 and that same policy might cost $60–$80/month, or be unavailable if health changes.
  • 🏢
    Employer vs. personalEmployer-provided life insurance is a nice perk (usually 1–2x salary), but it disappears when you leave the job. Always carry your own personal policy as your foundation.

Where to Keep Your Documents

Having the right documents means nothing if nobody can find them when it matters. Accessibility is part of the plan.

  • 🔒
    Fireproof safe at homeStore originals in a fireproof, waterproof safe. A bank safe deposit box works too, but access can be delayed after death, so check your state's rules.
  • 📑
    Give copies to trusted peopleYour executor, your guardian, and at least one other trusted person should have copies. Don't keep everything in one place only you can access.
  • 💻
    Digital access mattersUse a password manager and share master access with your executor or spouse. Include login info for bank accounts, insurance portals, email, and social media.
  • 📁
    Create a "what if" binderOne physical folder with: will location, insurance policy numbers, bank account list, financial advisor contact, attorney contact, digital passwords reference, and a letter of instruction. Label it clearly and tell someone where it is.
🎯
The one thing to do today: Tell your executor (or spouse, or trusted person) where your important documents are. If something happened tonight, could they find your will, insurance policies, and account information? If not, fix that first.
